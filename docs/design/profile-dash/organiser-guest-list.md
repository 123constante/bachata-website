## How you get there (both options)

The guest list belongs to a night, so you reach it from the night.

1. **Events** (`entry`): a night's row shows **Guest list &middot; 23 names, closes 22:00**, or **Off for this night**.
2. Tap the row, and the night sheet opens (`entrysheet`). Under **On the night**, the "Coming soon" Guest list row
   from the picked Events design is replaced by a real row: a **switch for this night only**, a line reading
   "On &middot; 23 names &middot; closes 22:00", and **Open the guest list**. Raffle keeps its "Tell us" row.
3. Open the guest list and you get a full screen with **&lsaquo; Events** at the top. The tab bar does not change.

The same night card on Home leads to the same night sheet. Home is not redrawn here.

## The two layout ideas

| | **A: one page per night** | **B: Names / Door / Settings** |
|---|---|---|
| Shape of the screen | One scroll: switch, then counts (names / places left / closes, or names / in / not yet on the night), then Add + Share link (Scan QR on the night), then names, then **Settings for this night**, then the public-names note | A small three-way switch under the night title: **Names 23**, **Door 41/63**, **Settings**. It opens on Door on the night and on Names before it |
| Adding a name | **Add a name** button, which opens a sheet (name, This night only / Every week) | Inline "Add a name" field + **Add** at the top of Names (the sheet then asks This night / Every week) |
| Door check-in | The same list gains a **Check in** button on every row; checked-in rows show a tick and **In &check;** | Its own Door view: a big "41 of 63 in" with a progress bar, a full-width **Scan a QR code**, an always-on search, and rows ordered **not in yet first** |
| Settings | Always at the bottom of the page | Their own view |
| Removing | Tap a name, then **Remove from the list**, then a confirm sheet (with an Undo toast after) | The same |
| 140 names | Search above the list, then 50 at a time with "Show 50 more (50 of 140 shown)" | The same, in both Names and Door |

Shared by both (one function each in `core.js`):
- the per-night switch and its wording;
- the status line and the state banners (full, closed, cancelled, past);
- the contributor reason;
- the add, person, remove, settings, link and QR sheets;
- the public-names note and the team-only variant;
- the dancer view;
- the loading and error states.

### Why A, in plain English
Everything for the night is on one page, so a first-time organiser can't get lost. They see the switch, the counts
and the names together, and "Check in" just appears on the night. It is the smaller build, too: one screen and
some sheets. The cost shows at the door. With 63 names, the counts, actions and settings take a screen of their
own, so finding the next person to tick means scrolling past people who are already in.

### Why B, in plain English
The organiser does two different jobs at two different times. Before the night they collect names; at the door
they tick people off fast with one hand. B gives each job its own view. Door opens by itself on the night, shows
the not-yet-arrived first, and puts the scanner and the search at the top. The cost is one more level of tabs
inside a tab, so a new organiser has to learn that "Settings" lives in a third view.

## Privacy: names are public (owner's choice), and the risk

The owner chose **public names** (2026-11-12). Both options draw it that way: the public note on every list says
so, and the dancer's button carries "Your name will be visible on this list."

**The risk, plainly:**
- A public guest list tells anyone, without signing in, **where a named person will be, on which night, and at
  what time**. For someone avoiding an ex or a stalker, that is a real safety problem.
- The list may also be indexed or scraped. Today's list holds first names only (`first_name`, inventory #3), which
  limits the harm. Linking each entry to an account (the "one entry per account" rule) would make names fuller and
  easier to identify unless the display is kept to a first name.

**What keeps the risk down while names stay public (all drawn):**
- the notice is on the button itself, not in a footnote;
- signing in is required;
- each account gets one entry;
- **Remove me** is always there;
- the list disappears from the public page when the night ends (today's behaviour, BentoPage.tsx:564-573).

**Not drawn, for the owner to decide:**
- show the first name only (as today), even when the account has a full name;
- add `noindex` to the list block;
- let a dancer join without their name showing ("+1 more").

**The alternative** is drawn as `teamonly` / `&v=team` on BOTH options. It is a clearly marked variant, not a
substitute: the public page shows only the count, and the dancer sees their own name and nobody else's.

## Careful lane (admin repo: DB / RPC / ACL changes needed before either option ships)

1. **C1 The per-night switch, places and closing time.** `has_guestlist`, `guest_list_capacity_max` and
   `guest_list_config` are **series** columns today (6 series on). A per-night switch needs occurrence-level storage:
   either a per-occurrence override row, or columns on `event_occurrence_p5`. It also needs a resolver that
   `get_event_guest_list` and `submit_guest_list_entry` both read. This is the biggest item, and it must not flip
   the 6 live series.
2. **C2 One entry per account.** Entries have no user id. Needed: a `user_id` (or person id) column, a unique
   constraint per (user, night), and a submit RPC that requires `auth.uid()` (today it is anonymous). Deciding what
   happens to the 593 existing anonymous rows is part of this.
3. **C3 Signed-in sign-up and remove-me.** A new `remove_my_guest_list_entry_v1` (by `auth.uid()`). It must sit
   alongside the token erase path (`dancer_erase_guest_entry_v1`), not replace it.
4. **C4 Door check-in.** No checked-in column exists. Needed: `checked_in_at` / `checked_in_by` on entries, plus
   `organiser_check_in_guest_v1` / undo. Realtime already listens to UPDATE (useGuestListRealtime.ts:81-103).
   `_guest_list_door_headcount_v1` may already be the count.
5. **C5 "Added by" and "every week".** No `added_by` column. Organiser-added `is_permanent` names need an organiser
   path (today only admin can add them, and only by first name).
6. **C6 Organiser-scoped RPCs.** Every list, add, remove, restore, config-save and check-in RPC is `admin_*` today.
   Organiser versions are needed, gated by team role: owner and manager can change; contributor gets read-only (and
   does a contributor get check-in? That is an ACL decision, see the questions below). Each must page past 100 rows.
7. **C7 Self sign-up link.** A per-night short link that opens the sign-up, and stops at close or when full.
8. **C8 QR check-in.** `qr_token` exists on entries but is unused. Needed: the dancer's QR display, a scan RPC that
   checks the night and the organiser's seat, and the "code for another night" error.
9. **C9 Team-only names (if the owner switches to the variant).** `get_event_guest_list` must stop returning other
   people's names to the public; the count stays.

## Recommendation (not a pick)

**Recommend Option B**, by a small margin. The real nights are mostly small (median 9 names), so either option
reads well before the night. The difference shows at the door. The night with 63 names is exactly where A makes
the organiser scroll past the counts and the people already in, while B opens on Door with the not-yet-arrived
first and the scanner at the top. One-handed, at a door, with a queue, that matters. If the owner values a single
simple screen over door speed, A is the safer first build: it is one screen, and B's Door view could be added later
without changing anything else.

**Alternatives rejected:**
1. A separate Guest list tab in the bottom bar. The brief keeps the bar unchanged, and the list belongs to a night.
2. Swipe to check in or to remove. It is hidden, and a mis-swipe at a busy door is costly.
3. One table with a column per night for a series. It is cramped at 390px and mixes nights that each have their own switch.
