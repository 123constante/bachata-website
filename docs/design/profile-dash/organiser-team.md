## Public page vs what the team sees (owner rule 2026-11-07, not reopened)

Both options say it the same way: owners and managers always show ("Shown on your public page", sheet:
"Always shown"); contributors have a per-person "Show on our public page" switch; an unclaimed stub shows
"Not claimed yet, hidden from your public page until they claim it" and has no switch. The team sees
everyone, including hidden and unclaimed people.

**Honest gap:** today's code does NOT enforce "owners and managers always show". `edit-in-place/teamHide.ts:27-33`
lets an owner hide anyone (owners included) and a manager hide anyone but an owner, and the per-row switch
in `team/index.tsx:54-110` follows that. The designs draw the owner rule; making the code match it is a
careful-lane change (below).

## Careful lane (needs a DB or ACL change in the admin repo before either option ships as drawn)

1. **Team invite links with a role and expiry.** `organiser_invites` has `member_role` and `expires_at`, but
   every row today is an owner claim invite; a manager / contributor team invite, its redeem path and a
   "list waiting invites" read are new RPCs.
2. **Invite by email.** No UI or send path; `organiser_invite_queue` exists with 0 rows. Needs the
   send RPC + mailer, "send again" and "cancel".
3. **Role changes beyond Make Manager** (demote, make owner, contributor) on `organiser_team_set_member_v1`.
4. **Contributor seats.** 0 in prod; what a contributor may read (Home numbers are owner/manager only
   today) needs an ACL decision. The role lines in both files are draft copy until then.
5. **"Owners and managers always show publicly"** must be enforced server-side and in `teamHide.ts`.
6. **Claim link for an unclaimed stub** from the Team tab (today claim invites are admin-issued).

## Recommendation: **Option B** (picked by the owner 2026-11-12)

Plain English: B answers the question people actually have on this tab, "who can do what?", without
a separate key, because each group says what its role can do right where the names are. Prod pages
mostly have 1 or 2 people, and B still reads well there (an "Only you so far" card and one Add button);
at 12 people the folds keep it short. (Fix round: Waiting invites now sit after Contributors, capped at 2.)
A is simpler to build and puts adding front and centre, which suits a brand-new page, but its role key
sits below the list, so the chip on a row and its meaning are a scroll apart, and at 12 people the add
card plus search push the team down.

**Alternatives rejected:** (1) a table with a role dropdown on every row: fast for admins but invites
accidental role changes and is cramped at 390px; (2) swipe-to-remove rows: hidden, and removal must
confirm and explain anyway; (3) a separate Invites tab: splits one small job across two places;
(4) A with group headings (a hybrid): ends up as B with an extra card.
